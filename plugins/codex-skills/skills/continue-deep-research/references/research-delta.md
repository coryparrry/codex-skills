# Research delta contract

Use the smallest structure that makes the change from the supplied research unmistakable.

## 1. Answer

Answer the central question immediately. State confidence and the most important qualification.

## 2. Baseline recovered

Briefly state the prior position, materials inspected, scope, and date or version boundary. Do not repeat the entire research packet.

## 3. Delta

Use only the categories that contain material findings:

| Category | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Retained | Strong prior findings that remain valid |
| Confirmed | Prior uncertainties resolved by direct evidence |
| Corrected | Stale, inaccurate, overstated, or wrongly scoped claims |
| New | Decision-relevant findings absent from the packet |
| Contradicted | Credible evidence for competing interpretations |
| Unresolved | Open questions, missing evidence, and why they matter |

For every material delta item, include:

- the precise claim;
- the new evidence and direct citation;
- relevant date, version, platform, jurisdiction, or population;
- confidence and the reason;
- how it changes or preserves the prior conclusion.

## 4. Implications

Translate the delta into the user's actual use case: code review, technical decision, document, policy, plan, explanation, or next investigation. Separate evidence from recommendation.

## 5. Evidence ledger

Include a compact ledger when the research contains many claims or disputed sources:

| Claim | Status | Best evidence | Scope/date | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not use the ledger as a substitute for explaining decisive conflicts.

## 6. Remaining research

List only follow-ups that could materially change the answer. For each, state the evidence needed and why it is not already available.

## Citation rules

- Place citations next to the supported claim.
- Link to the exact source page, proposal, issue, pull request, document, or dataset when possible.
- Preserve useful citations from the supplied research, but label them unverified until inspected.
- Paraphrase sources and quote only short, necessary excerpts.
- Do not cite search result pages or a secondary summary when the primary source is accessible.
